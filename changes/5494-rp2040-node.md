In short:

- **A Raspberry Pi Pico in a System:** the System editor's Pico tool places one, its program a `.py`, run on
  MicroPython's own firmware in rp2040js (downloaded once by the local server); what it prints is its REPL's terminal, and
  its UART links to the other nodes, simulated together in Try it.

In detail:

- **The RP2040 node (#5494):** `core/system-sim-rp2040.js`, rp2040js (MIT) on MicroPython v1.24.1 and the RP2040's
  bootrom, behind the node contract (its 27 pins at 3.3 V, its fidelity notes), its time the scheduler's; its program typed
  into the raw REPL as its 512-byte USB FIFO takes it, its console a terminal (`<id>.repl`), a traceback its error; its UARTs' pins as frames (rp2040js's TX
  interrupt made an edge, as the PL011's, for MicroPython's interrupt-driven write). The firmware is not shipped:
  `core/rp2040-firmware.js` pins its addresses and SHA-256, `server/rp2040-firmware.js` downloads it once and serves it at
  `/vendor/rp2040/` (the bootrom's mufplib is for the RP2040 alone or GPL-2.0: NOTICE and THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES). The board
  `pico` in the System document, its check (MicroPython only), the editor's tool and its Program (.py) field, the build plan
  (run by its REPL), the MCP's `edit_system`, `check_system` and `simulate`.
